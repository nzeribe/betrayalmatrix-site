The mathematics

This is not opinion with confidence. It is an engine — built on the real mathematics of strategic conflict, the field that modelled nuclear deterrence and runs the auctions behind the modern economy. A century of Nobel-winning work, each piece doing a job inside it:

  • John von Neumann founded game theory — what rational players do when their fates are entangled.
  • John Nash — yes, A Beautiful Mind — gave it the equilibrium: what happens when both people optimise against each other, not just one.
  • Thomas Schelling, who modelled the Cold War, gave it leverage — why a threat held beats a threat fired.
  • Gale & Shapley gave it matching markets — why your options and hers are never symmetric.
  • Michael Spence gave it signalling — why the grand gesture so often means the opposite of what it shows.

On top of that public mathematics sits the part that is ours alone: a proprietary model — its mechanisms, its weights, the rule that decides the verdict — grounded in verified research and kept private. You see what it concludes. You never see the engine.

Why an engine, and not advice? Because an engine can be wrong — and a thing that can be wrong is a thing that can be right. It explains what already happened and predicts what comes next, and can be tested against both. Advice can do neither.
